# Reusable manager review design

The reusable procedures derive project rules and consequential decision categories from current authority. They supply evidence operations, causal analysis and an attention filter. Project vocabulary, acceptance rules and exceptional procedures belong in the campaign's authoritative sources.

The revised files are [evaluation](manager-candidates/PROMPT_EVALUATE.md), [synthesis](manager-candidates/PROMPT_SYNTHESIZE.md), [campaign](manager-candidates/CAMPAIGN.md) and [protocol overview](manager-candidates/README.md). The source-grounded [Terra-high4 regression](TERRA_HIGH4_REGRESSION.md) is separate from the reusable procedures and from discovery-test inputs.

## Replacement mechanism

Evaluation first creates an **acceptance basis** and a **salience register**. Each salience entry cites authority and records its project-native category, significance, triggers, affected assumptions, prescribed process and decision owner. An unfamiliar worker proposal is not inherently high-salience, and high salience does not automatically require approval or notification.

Evidence touching a derived category receives causal review: concrete pressure, authoritative versus local constraints, necessity, ordinary and sibling alternatives, hidden consequences, residual upstream value and disposition. Cross-experiment evidence challenges initial independent assessments before findings are selected.

The attention filter suppresses avoidable local choices, unsupported necessity claims, routine corrections, answered questions and duplicates. It retains their dispositions in REVIEW_TRACE.md. A failed proposal can still yield a different useful finding, but that residual concern must survive alternatives and counterevidence and justify human attention. Explicit source-required disclosures remain recorded; they are not endorsements of the proposal.

The evaluator emits EVALUATION.md for assessed results, REVIEW_TRACE.md for the causal audit, and HUMAN_FINDINGS.md for vetted residual concerns and required disclosures. Synthesis preserves that filter, re-applies it to new concerns and prevents suppressed proposals or unresolved obligations from returning through optional guidance.

## V5 derivation and regression result

The v5 PROJECT marks additional deployer test interfaces as last-resort, requires a deferred fresh-context alternative search and permits later human rejection or promotion into the product. The stable interface guidance explains the review inventory and explicitly says workers need no separate approval workflow to continue. Those sources derive the relevant high-salience class and its actual procedure. The generic prompts contain neither that class nor those steps.

High4's two established mechanisms touch the derived class. Causal analysis does not forward either mechanism for approval merely because it appears in the inventory. The root mechanism's necessity is unproved; ordinary mounted state has sibling evidence, and the worker's chosen launch arrangement is not a universal product constraint. The finalization mechanism identifies a real timing problem, but its necessity and proof remain open to earlier observation alternatives.

The root review also exposes a material residual concern: project-required parallel runs on one Docker daemon meet product-wide command cleanup protected by a single daemon lock. Root separation does not scope that cleanup. The vetted finding asks the appropriate owners to clarify the isolation/ownership boundary before settling an interface. It does not recommend the paired-root mechanism or claim ordinary mounts are a complete solution. The [regression report](TERRA_HIGH4_REGRESSION.md) supplies the full evidence trace, suppression decisions and human-facing wording.

## Verification limits

Both reusable prompts were reviewed for domain-specific acceptance rules and scanned for the named project technologies and concepts. Project-specific vocabulary remains in the campaign and regression evidence. File references were checked separately.

This is a source-grounded regression by a reviewer who previously saw the archive and earlier conclusions. It demonstrates how the procedure represents and vets the evidence; it does not prove blind discovery or empirical reliability.
